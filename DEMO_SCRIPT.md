# Demo video script (~2 min) — paste the narration into ElevenLabs, record the screen in parallel

| # | Screen (app tab / action) | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overview, top | Apetit's climate and nature data is scattered across ERP lines, meters, farm surveys and supplier files, and the raw numbers can't be trusted. The Climate and Nature Navigator turns them into decisions an expert can approve. |
| 2 | Data quality → "Draft 2025 report vs cleaned" | The draft 2025 report overstates Scope 3 by three hundred times. Kilowatt-hours were read as megawatt-hours and tonnes were keyed as kilograms. Our data-quality agent finds these errors with eighteen detectors. On the public answer keys it scores ninety-six, against fifty-two for the baseline. |
| 3 | Issue queue: type reviewer name, click ✓ on one flag | Every flag comes with its evidence and a proposed fix. The agent proposes and the expert decides. Each decision is logged with a name and a timestamp. |
| 4 | GHG inventory, click cat01 | The cleaned 2025 inventory follows the GHG Protocol, and every line shows the factor and source behind it. Contract farming comes out three times higher once drained peat soils are counted. Today's calculator leaves out fifty-nine percent of farm emissions. |
| 5 | SBTi pathways: switch scenario, hover MACC | We set a 2025 base year with a full year of Foodhills. FLAG is seventy percent of emissions, so it needs its own target. Under seven NGFS scenarios, biogas and refrigerant changes meet the Scope 1+2 target. The rapeseed supplier programme and moving contract area off peat are the cheapest large levers. Structural changes and better data are never counted as reductions. |
| 6 | Climate risk: SSP buttons | The yield models learn drought, heat and disease responses from fifteen seasons of data. Weather risk is already here: the Finnish pea crop fails about one year in seven, costing almost half a million euros a year on average, and a forty-thousand-draw Monte Carlo shows the risk persisting to 2060. Sourcing peas from Finland and Sweden cuts the expected shortfall cost by up to seventy-two percent. |
| 7 | Nature: map hover, hotspot table | For nature, we follow phosphorus field by field to the water. Fifty fields carry fifteen percent of the load. The cheapest fixes cost under seventy euros per kilo of phosphorus, and an agronomist reviews each one. |
| 8 | Copilot: ask "Is soy protein isolate in EUDR scope?" | The copilot is a Claude agent with tools. It queries our own tables, answers in any language, cites its evidence and can draft the board memo. It never invents numbers. |
| 8b | Simulator: click "Auto-close the gap" | Then the what-if simulator: one click picks the cheapest levers to close every SBTi gap. It shows that FLAG needs the off-peat and rapeseed programmes roughly doubled, at an average of thirty-nine euros per tonne. |
| 9 | Overview → actions; click Approve | The result is seven actions with owners, tonnes and euros. Nothing is published until an expert approves it. Every step runs again from the raw data in two minutes, and it maps onto Apetit's Microsoft Fabric stack. |
